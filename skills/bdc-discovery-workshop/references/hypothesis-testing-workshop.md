# Hypothesis Testing Workshop

Use this reference when the founder wants to test whether they are solving the right problem before building.

## Facilitation Rules

- Run one stage at a time.
- End each stage with a one-line summary of what the founder committed to.
- If the founder describes demographics, market size, or a product feature, redirect to a specific person in a specific moment.
- If the founder says users do nothing today, challenge it. Ignoring, delaying, using spreadsheets, asking a friend, or tolerating pain are all current alternatives.
- Flag unsupported pain claims. Pain level needs evidence from behavior, money, time, urgency, or repeated conversations.
- Require a measurable threshold and a kill condition before calling anything a hypothesis.
- Push toward two-day learning tests, not product builds.

## Stage Checklist

1. The scene: a real person in a real moment.
2. Progress: a job statement in the form "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."
3. Current alternative: what they do today.
4. Pain: score, evidence, and switching difficulty.
5. Riskiest belief: the assumption that collapses the idea if false.
6. Hypothesis: user, behavior, threshold, timeframe, and kill condition.
7. Smallest test: the fastest way to learn within two days.

Begin with Stage 1. Do not preamble.

For each stage, ask only what is needed to complete that stage. If the founder gives a weak answer, ask for specificity before advancing.

## Output Format

At the end, produce a paste-ready block:

```markdown
## Hypothesis

We believe [specific user] will [behavior] because [root cause].
We'll know we're right if [measurable threshold within timeframe].
We'll know we're wrong if [kill condition].

## Two-Day Test

Test:
Target participants:
Steps:
Evidence collected:
Decision rule:
```

Then offer to convert the result into a row-ready testing-log record.
